Honours thesis, 2025–2026

How an eggplant soaks up oil: one structure, measured three independent ways

I characterised the mesocarp of Solanum melongena L. as a natural oil sorbent for environmental remediation. The question was whether its internal pore structure explains how much oil it can absorb and how fast fluid moves through it.

I didn't trust any single method on its own, so I built three that don't share assumptions. Each one checks the others.

  • Image analysisPython/OpenCV pipeline (Otsu thresholding, connected-component labelling) across 59 cross-sections: 4,498 pores, 22.2% porosity, 954 µm mean pore diameter, permeability 9.16 × 10⁻¹¹ m².
  • CFD3D porous-zone model in ANSYS Fluent (Darcy–Forchheimer, laminar). Permeability from the simulated pressure drop landed within 0.17% of the image-derived value.
  • Physical testingGravimetric absorption on 26 specimens in 4 fluids. Dehydrated samples reached 1.69 g/g, 96.6% of the theoretical maximum.

The framework works for any agricultural biosorbent. It also leads straight into my MSc, where the same pore geometry becomes the input for Lattice Boltzmann thermal-storage simulations.
